Main project

IDEAM Station Data Downloader

Colombia's national weather and hydrology institute (IDEAM) publishes decades of station records through its DHIME portal, but the portal hands them out one station and one query at a time. A watershed study can need 40 or 200 stations, which means hours of repetitive clicking and a pile of spreadsheets to merge by hand.

I designed and built a web app that does the whole job from a map: you define the study area, it finds every station inside it, estimates how much data each one really has for your period, and returns one organized ZIP with merged Excel files, a summary table and the source citation.

9,857stations in the national catalog, screened spatially in seconds
4input formats for the study area: Shapefile (ZIP), GeoJSON, KML/KMZ, GeoPackage
−6% to +3%error of the download-size estimate, measured against real downloads
~7,800lines of Python and JavaScript, built and refined over 3 weeks

What I did

The GIS and data work behind it

Buffers in the right projection

Uploaded boundaries are checked for missing or wrong coordinate systems, invalid geometries are repaired, and buffers are computed in a metric projection (MAGNA-SIRGAS, EPSG:3116) before returning to WGS84, so a 2 km buffer is really 2 km.

Spatial joins and attribute filters

Stations are selected with a point-in-polygon join against the area plus buffer, or by department, municipality and station code. Duplicates from multi-part polygons are removed.

Screening before the heavy work

Each station gets an estimated coverage for the requested period and a quality class, shown on the map and in a checklist. Users decide what to download before spending time on it, which is the same logic as a site pre-screen.

Cross-checking sources

The catalog altitude of every station is compared with a terrain model. Stations whose recorded altitude does not match the ground are flagged for review instead of being silently trusted.

Working with an undocumented public API

I mapped how the portal requests data, split long periods into year blocks, merged the returned spreadsheets into one per station, and capped concurrent requests at 4 so the public service is not overloaded.

Sourced, organized outputs

Every download includes a summary table and a citation file in the format the data owner requires. Folders can be split by coverage class so the best data is easy to find.

How I would run a first site screen for KND Fuels

  1. Map the corridor and the competition

    Interstates I-10, I-17, I-40, I-8 and I-19, their interchanges, and every existing truck stop and diesel station along them. Stretches far from any truck stop are the first gaps to look at.

  2. Score each interchange

    Truck traffic on the adjacent segment, distance to the nearest competitor, and population within 15 and 30 miles. A simple weighted score ranks the candidates and shows why each one ranks where it does.

  3. Screen land near the best ones

    Parcels within about a mile of the ramp: acreage, owner, zoning and access from county assessor records, and land manager (private, State Trust, federal, tribal), which matters a lot in Arizona.

  4. Flag constraints

    FEMA flood zones, environmental records and access problems, each marked for follow-up with a link to its source.

  5. Deliver a short package

    A map, a tracker in Excel or Google Sheets, and one page per site with key findings, pros and cons and a recommendation for further review.
